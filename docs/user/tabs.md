# Tabs

> **Status:** available from 0.1.0-beta.6.

## What a tab shows

- **The icon of its page,** or an icon for the browser's own pages, such as the settings and the history. Astela loads the icon of a page without cookies and shows icons of up to 100 KB in the formats PNG, ICO, GIF, JPEG, WebP, BMP and SVG; a page without one shows a globe.
- **A turning ring** while the page loads. When the system asks for reduced motion, the ring stands still.
- **A speaker** while the page plays sound. Click it to mute the tab, and click it again to turn the sound on. A muted tab stays muted when it opens another page, until you turn its sound on or close it.

## Moving tabs

Drag a tab along the tab strip to move it; the other tabs make room for it, and it lands where you let go. Dragging a tab does not switch to it. Tabs open, close and move with a short animation, which the system's setting for reduced motion turns off.

## Many tabs

Tabs get narrower as you open more of them. Narrow tabs show only the icon of their page, and the active tab shows its close button instead; a middle click closes any tab. When the tabs no longer fit, the tab strip scrolls, also with the mouse wheel, and a line marks each side behind which tabs are hidden.

## Pinned tabs

Pin a tab from its menu to keep it at the start of the tab strip, where it shows only the icon of its page. Pinned tabs stay pinned at the next start, keep their place before the other tabs when you move tabs, and have no close button: close them from their menu or with the shortcut. In a tab strip at a side, pinned tabs keep their titles.

## The menu of a tab

A click with the secondary button on a tab opens its menu: a new tab next to it, reload, duplicate, pin or unpin, mute or unmute, close, close the other tabs and close the tabs to the right, which leave pinned tabs open, and reopen the last closed tab.

## Keyboard

`Ctrl+1` to `Ctrl+8` (`Command+1` to `Command+8` on macOS) switch to the tab at that place, and `Ctrl+9` (`Command+9`) to the last tab. On Windows and Linux, `Ctrl+Shift+Page Up` and `Ctrl+Shift+Page Down` move the active tab. The settings can give these commands, and pinning a tab, shortcuts of your own; see [Keyboard shortcuts](keyboard-shortcuts.md).
