# 0055. Tab groups and workspaces in the tab model, with a partition per isolated workspace

## Status

Accepted. Deviates from [ADR 0010](0010-module-system-and-lazy-loading.md): groups and workspaces are core state of the tabs, with chunks of their own that load with their first use, instead of feature modules; `workspaces.enabled` with the policy `WorkspacesEnabled` keeps the activation contract of ADR 0010. Extends [ADR 0040](0040-tabs-and-popups-that-keep-their-opener.md), [ADR 0052](0052-command-palette-and-tab-search-in-the-overlay.md), [ADR 0053](0053-sleeping-tabs-and-an-exact-session.md) and [ADR 0054](0054-split-view-with-two-tabs-side-by-side.md), and decides what [ADR 0002](0002-electron-and-webcontentsview.md), [ADR 0013](0013-theme-system-and-design-tokens.md), [ADR 0041](0041-private-windows-with-a-session-in-memory.md) and [ADR 0043](0043-themes-as-palettes-edited-on-the-settings-page.md) leave open for groups and workspaces. Tab groups are built in 0.1.0-beta.7: groups from the menu of a tab, their chips and lines at every place of the tab strip, their popover, collapsed groups, the tabs that a grouped tab opens, moves into and out of groups by drag and keyboard, and groups after a restart. Workspaces are built in 0.1.0-beta.7 as well: the list of workspaces at their button, switching with the list and the shortcuts, renaming, moving and deleting workspaces, moving tabs between them from the menu of a tab and by drag, partitions of their own for isolated workspaces, the setting and policy that turn them off, the group and the workspace of each tab in tab search, and the workspaces after a restart. Themes per workspace move to 0.1.0-beta.8.

## Date

2026-11-11

## Context

Until 0.1.0-beta.6, `TabService` kept the tabs of a window in one list; the tab foundations of 0.1.0-beta.7 moved them into a `TabStrip`, which knows pinned tabs and the pairs of split views as units. People with many tabs want to sort them into named, colored groups that they can hide, as in Chrome, Edge and Firefox, and to keep sets of tabs apart for work, a project or private errands, as Arc, Vivaldi and Firefox's containers do with workspaces, some of them with cookies and sign-ins of their own.

ADR 0010 plans every optional feature as a module in `app/src/modules/<id>/` with a manifest. That host does not exist yet: `bootstrap.ts` wires every feature itself, and every feature since 0.1.0-beta.1 loads its code for the browser process lazily from `app/src/main/*.ts`. Groups and workspaces are not features beside the tabs either: every start, every restore, every new tab and every move of a tab needs them, and the session keeps them.

The main start-up path of the browser process has 150 KiB, and 0.1.0-beta.7 has little left of it: each remaining part of the release may add at most 0.6 KiB. Placement rules, moves, keyboard steps, collapsing and closing of groups need several KiB once minified.

`session.json` stays at version 1, so that 0.1.0-beta.6 reads it with every tab after a rollback ([ADR 0053](0053-sleeping-tabs-and-an-exact-session.md)).

## Decision

### Tab groups

- **A group of the strip:** `TabGroup { id, name, color, collapsed }`. The id is `g` and a number in base 36, unique in its window; the name is trimmed and at most 50 characters, and may be empty; the color is one of the six swatches `blue`, `teal`, `green`, `orange`, `red` and `graphite` (`app/src/shared/swatches.ts`), the accent presets other than Astela's own, which `accentPresets` takes over. Each tab of a group carries the group (`Tab.group`), and a group exists only through its tabs: the last tab that leaves a group takes it along, so no group is ever empty.
- **The rules of a strip with groups** (`GroupedTabStrip` in `app/src/domain/tabs/tab-groups.ts`), checked after each of 500 random changes in the unit tests: the tabs of a group stand together; pinned tabs are never in a group, and pinning takes a tab out of its group; the active tab is never in a collapsed group; the two tabs of a split view are in one group, the tab that joins a split view taking the group of the tab that stays. A new group takes the color that the groups of the window use least.
- **Where new tabs go:** a new tab after a tab, as **New tab to the right** and **Duplicate** open it, joins the group of that tab. A tab that a page of a grouped tab opens, by a link, a middle click, the menu of the page or `window.open`, joins the group of its opener, after the opener and the tabs it opened in the background before; a tab that opens in the background without an opener counts as opened by the active tab. A new tab in front without an opener, from the New tab button, `Ctrl+T`, a link from another app or a reopened tab outside a group, goes after the active tab, or after the end of its group, outside every group. A tab of a group never opens between two tabs of another group.
- **Moves:** `move(id, index, group?)` takes the index without the tab, as before. A named group takes the place into its tabs; `null` keeps the tab outside every group, after a group whose tabs stand on both sides of the place; without a group, a place between two tabs of a group that shows its tabs joins that group, and a tab keeps its own group at its edges. A collapsed group moves as a whole and stays outside other groups, and no tab joins it by a move.
- **Keyboard:** `Ctrl+Shift+Page Up` and `Page Down` move the active tab one step: a tab at the edge of its group first leaves the group where it is, a tab next to a group that shows its tabs first joins it at that edge, and a collapsed group is passed, or passes, as a whole. `Ctrl+Tab`, `Ctrl+Page Up` and `Page Down` and `Ctrl+1` to `Ctrl+9` count only the tabs that the strip shows (`listed`). The command palette agrees: each tab of `CommandContext` carries its group, `canRun` counts the tabs that collapsed groups do not hide, and lets a tab of a group that shows its tabs move both ways, also at an end of the strip, where it leaves its group; a unit test runs `canRun` against `listed` and `step` through random strips.
- **Collapsing:** a collapsed group hides its tabs behind its chip, which shows how many they are. Collapsing the group of the active tab makes the nearest tab after the group active, or else the one before it, or opens a new tab after the group when no tab shows. Showing a tab of a collapsed group, from tab search, the suggestions of the address bar or a restore, opens its group.
- **Closing and reopening:** the popover's **Close group** closes the tabs of a group as one closed entry, which `Ctrl+Shift+T` opens again as a group with its name and color, its first tab loaded and in front and the others asleep until they show. A single tab that closed goes back into its group while the group is there.
- **The tab strip:** the chip of a group comes before its first tab, with a dot in the group's color and its name; each tab of the group has a 2 px line in that color on its edge that faces the page: the bottom edge with the strip at the top, the top edge with the strip at the bottom, and the start edge, with the tabs indented below a row of the chip with a chevron, at a side. The line is a border, so forced colors keep it. A click on the chip, Enter or Space shows or hides the tabs; its context menu, from the pointer, the context menu key or a Control-click, opens the popover. Enter and Space keep the focus on the chip, also when the active tab changes with it: `groups.toggle` says that the keyboard asked, the browser then runs the change through `BrowserWindowController.keepingFocus`, which gives the page of the tab that becomes active no focus, and the shell leaves the focus on the chip when a new tab page becomes active. A click gives the focus to the page of that tab, as a click on a tab does. A context menu without a button (`button` -1) is one of the keyboard: Chromium gives the context menu key and Shift+F10 the pointer type `mouse`, not the empty pointer type of the standard, which the shell takes as well. The colors are the tokens `--swatch-*` of `tokens.css`, in light, dark and private mode, at least 3:1 against every surface of every built-in theme. Screen readers say _In group …_ or _In a group without a name_ after the title of a tab, and _Tabs: n_ for a collapsed chip.
- **Dragging:** `dropGroup` of `app/src/ui/shell/strip-items.ts` gives the group that a dropped tab joins: the group that shows its tabs over whose span, from the start of its chip to the end of its last tab, with the tab's own place, the center of the tab lies. A tab dragged more than half its width past the first or last tab of its group leaves it; collapsed groups take no tab. Chips are items of the strip that make room like tabs, and the chip of a group that a tab would join shows it. While a window has groups, the shell names the group in `tabs.move`, also `null`.
- **The menu of a tab** (`app/src/main/tab-menu.ts`): **Add to new group** while the window has no other group, otherwise **Add to group** with **New group** and every other group, named by its name or by the title of its first tab; **Remove from group** for a grouped tab, which puts it right after its group. Pinned tabs get none of them. The names of groups and the titles of pages are no access keys: `&` in them is written `&&` on every system, as the native menus of macOS also hide an `&` and show `&&` as one. A title, or the address of a page without one, shows with at most its first 300 characters and an ellipsis, as titles do in the menus of bookmarks (`labelLimit`), without splitting a character.
- **The popover** (`app/src/ui/overlay/group-popover.ts`, `app/src/main/tab-groups.ts`) opens at the chip in the overlay, below it, or above it with the strip at the bottom: the name, which changes while people type, the color as six round radio buttons named by the accent colors of the settings, **New tab in group**, **Ungroup** and **Close group**. Escape and a click next to it close it. After **Add to new group**, the browser asks the shell with `shell.editGroup` to open it at the new chip, with the empty name field focused; when the context menu key or Shift+F10 opened the menu of the tab, which `tabs.openMenu` tells, the event says so, and the focus goes back to the tab strip when the popover closes, as after a popover that the keyboard opened at its button. It closes when its group is gone, and does not give way to questions of sites (`yieldsToQuestions: false`).
- **Session:** each tab of a group keeps its group, `{ id, name, color, collapsed }`, in the optional field `group`, read field by field; a group that cannot be read costs only itself. After a restart, neighbouring tabs that kept the same group form it again with a new id, collapsed when it was; pinned tabs never do. The code of groups loads before the windows open only when the session has a group.
- **Lazy:** the start-up path keeps the field of the tab, of the shell's state and of the session, the opener of tabs that pages open, two generic operations of `TabService`, `keepingFocus` of the window and the names of the shell's two channels. A window gets the rules of groups with its first group: `TabService.useStrip` puts a `GroupedTabStrip` in the place of the plain strip, with its tabs, pairs and closed tabs, and `TabService.edit(change)` runs each change of groups and tells the views and the session, closing the views of tabs that left and showing what the strip shows now. A strip with groups places and moves tabs as the plain strip does while it has none, which a unit test checks. The menu items, the popover, the handlers of `groups.toggle`, `groups.openEditor` and of moves with a group, and the domain code load with their first use.

### Workspaces

- **Definitions per profile, tabs per window:** the workspaces are the profile setting `workspaces.list`, 1 to 20 entries `{ id, name, icon, color, isolated }` (`app/src/shared/workspaces.ts`), among them the default workspace `default`, first in a new profile, which can be renamed, recolored, moved and given another icon but never deleted or isolated. The id of another workspace is `w` and up to 15 digits and letters of base 36, its name is trimmed, at most 40 characters and never empty, its icon one of 16 (`app/src/shared/workspace-icons.ts`) and its color one of the six swatches of groups. The setting has no row on the settings page and is neither exported nor imported there, so that an import never removes a workspace that has tabs. Each window keeps the tabs of each workspace in a `TabStrip` of its own and shows one workspace at a time; a switch detaches the views of one strip and attaches those of another, so nothing reloads, and only the views of the shown strip are ever shown. Tab ids stay unique across the strips of a window.
- **The strips of a window** (`WorkspaceStrips` in `app/src/domain/tabs/workspace-strips.ts`) take the place of the window's plain strip, through `TabService.useStrip`, with its second workspace. What concerns the window, such as the active tab, the listed tabs, the closed tabs and a new tab, is the shown strip's; what concerns one tab, such as its title, its split view, a move or its closing, is the strip's that holds it, also while it is hidden. A tab that a page or a tab opens goes into the strip of that tab. Activating a tab of a hidden strip makes it that strip's active tab without showing it; `revealTab` shows it, as tab search and the suggestions of the address bar do. A workspace that tabs moved into before it ever showed shows the first of them, a workspace without tabs a new tab page.
- **The button and the list:** the first item of the tab strip is the button of the workspaces, with the icon of the workspace that shows in its color, and its name in a strip at a side; private windows and popups have none. It opens the list (`app/src/ui/overlay/workspace-list.ts`) in the overlay, below it or above it, at its start: each workspace with its icon, name, number of tabs in this window, whether one plays sound, whether it keeps its cookies apart, its shortcut and a button to edit it, and **New workspace**. The form names a workspace and chooses its icon and its color; a new one chooses whether it keeps its cookies and sign-ins apart, an existing one moves up or down and, but for the default one, is deleted after a question. A switch says the name of the workspace to screen readers.
- **Turning workspaces off:** `workspaces.enabled` with the policy `WorkspacesEnabled`, mandatory or recommended, keeps the activation contract of ADR 0010, with a row after `splitView.enabled` on the settings page. Off, every window's strips merge into the default workspace, at the start and live, workspace by workspace, each group and split view with the tabs of its own workspace; the button, the commands and their shortcuts of the workspaces are gone, and the menu of a tab has no item to move it. On again, every tab stays in the default workspace. That holds also before the code of the workspaces has loaded, as after a restart that brought back several workspaces: a change of `workspaces.enabled` or `workspaces.list` loads that code at once while a window has tabs in another workspace than the default one or shows another one, and its first use brings every window in line with the settings; from then on it follows their changes itself.
- **Isolation:** each isolated workspace loads its pages in the partition `persist:ws-<id>`, which gets the same hardening as `persist:default` through `protectWebSession`, which every session that Electron makes passes: the content blocker, the permission handler and the refusal of `app://`. Isolation is chosen when a workspace is made and never changes. History, bookmarks, the list of downloads, the permissions of sites and the zoom of sites stay shared, as with Firefox's containers; clearing browsing data and the end of the browser's run, which writes the cookies to disk, cover every workspace, and a download tries again in the partition that it came from. A partition opens on the way to the first tab of its workspace that shows; a window looks up the icons of its tabs only in the sessions that its views used, so that no icon opens the partition of a workspace whose tabs did not load.
- **Deleting:** deleting a workspace closes its tabs in every window and its popups, shows the default workspace in the windows that showed it, and removes its definition with its overrides of settings (`SettingsService.clearWorkspace`). An isolated workspace's storage, cache and sign-ins are cleared at once, and its id is kept in `workspaces.removed`, so that its folder `<userData>/Partitions/ws-<id>` goes at the next start, after the first shell loaded, when Chromium no longer holds its files; only valid ids inside `Partitions` are removed, and a new workspace never takes such an id.
- **Moving a tab:** the menu of a tab has **Move to workspace** with every other workspace, and a dragged tab that points at the button of the workspaces shows a target for each other workspace over the start of the tabs, which fade in with `--motion-fast`; the targets are found by their rectangles, and a drop sends `workspaces.moveTab`. The tab goes to the end of the pinned tabs or of the other tabs there, outside every group, and leaves its split view first. A tab that moves into a workspace with another partition gets a new view, which restores its back and forward entries without page state through the dormant tab of ADR 0053, and loads again once it shows; the item and the target say so. Within one partition, the view moves as it is.
- **Popups and private windows:** what a page opens, a popup included, stays in the workspace and partition of that page, even a hidden one, and never switches the window's workspace. Private windows and popups have no workspaces; links from other apps open in the workspace of the window used last, and new windows show the workspace of the window they came from.
- **Shortcuts:** `Alt+1` to `Alt+9` on Windows and Linux, `Control+1` to `Control+9` on macOS, show the first to ninth workspace; the next and previous workspace, the list of workspaces and `tab.newGroup`, which puts the active tab in a new group, come as commands without a default shortcut: `tab.newGroup` after the commands of split view, and the commands of workspaces before `page.back` in the catalog. Never Ctrl+Alt or Option: Windows reports AltGr as Ctrl+Alt (`shortcutProblem` refuses it as `altgr`), and Option types characters on macOS. Alt with a digit of the number pad stays the Alt codes of Windows; without Num Lock, the pad sends arrows and the like, which keep their names, so that Alt and its arrows go back and forward. Private windows and popups, which have no workspaces, leave these keys to their pages.
- **Session:** `session.json` stays at version 1. Tabs get the optional field `workspace`, written strip by strip in the order of `workspaces.list`, the active tab of each hidden workspace the mark `current`, and windows the workspace that they show; `active` is the active tab of the shown workspace, so 0.1.0-beta.6 opens every tab with the right one active. Only the active tab of the shown workspace loads at a restart.
- **Split views, groups and workspaces:** the two tabs of a split view are always in one group and one workspace. Moving one of them into another workspace ends the split view first; hiding the group of the split view that shows makes the nearest tab outside it active; dragging a group carries a split view whole; a switch shows the active tab of the workspace with its split view. Turning split view off ends the split views of every workspace, not only those of the workspaces that show.
- **Tab search** lists the tabs of every workspace of the window, each with its group and, while the profile has more than one workspace, its workspace; choosing a tab of another workspace shows that workspace, as the suggestions of the address bar do. Both make a tab of the workspace that shows the active tab through `TabService.activate`, and load `revealTab` with the strips of the workspaces only for a tab of another workspace (`showTab` of `app/src/domain/tabs/show-tab.ts`), which only a window with workspaces has.
- **Themes per workspace:** a workspace may get a theme and an accent of its own, as settings with the scope `workspace` of `ThemeSetting` and `AccentSetting`; that step moves to 0.1.0-beta.8.
- **Lazy:** the list, the forms, the switch, the commands, the moves and the deletion load as `app/src/main/workspaces.ts`, the strips per workspace and their operations as `app/src/domain/tabs/workspace-strips.ts`, and the list operations as `app/src/domain/workspaces/workspaces.ts`; the strips extend `TabService` with the same two operations that groups use. The start-up path keeps the workspace of each strip and the lookups of tabs by id in `TabService`, the fields of the session, of the shell's state and of the settings, the commands and their shortcuts, the partition of each tab and the wiring of `bootstrap.ts`. The menu of a tab loads the code of the workspaces only in a window with another workspace to move the tab into.

### Deviations from the design

- The design numbered this decision 0049, and the plan of 0.1.0-beta.7 0054; ADR 0049 for the logo shifted the numbers.
- The rules of groups live in `GroupedTabStrip`, a subclass of `TabStrip` in the chunk of groups, which takes the place of a window's plain strip with its first group, not in `TabStrip` itself; and the operations of groups are methods of that strip, run through `editGroups` and `closeGroup` of `tab-groups.ts` with `TabService.edit`, not methods of `TabService`. Placement, moves, steps, collapsing and closing of groups take several KiB, and this part of the release may add only 0.6 KiB to the start-up path; so does `useStrip` keep the windows without groups from ever loading them.
- `Tab.group` and `TabView.group` hold the whole group rather than its id, and the shell takes its chips from the tabs; `ShellState` has no list of groups. The session keeps the group with each tab, not ids with a list of groups per window. One field instead of two needs less code on the start-up path, and leaves no reference to a group that is not there.
- `TabStrip.reopen()` became `reopenWith(open)`, which opens a closed tab through `TabService.open`, so that a closed group opens again with all its tabs.
- `TabService.close` goes through `edit` as well, and `edit` replaces only an active tab that its change took away, so that a restore forms its groups before its first tab is active.
- The valibot schema `SessionSnapshot`, which only gave the type of the session, became types: valibot's calls are no pure functions for the minifier, so the schema stayed in the start-up path as dead code. That paid for most of the groups: the start-up path, walked with the chunks that it imports, is 137.9 KiB with them, 0.8 KiB more than before this part, 0.2 KiB more than its share, with the chunks that the code of groups shares with the start.
- A tab that a page opens joins the group of its opener once the window has groups; a tab from a tab outside every group goes where it went without groups, after the active tab, also when the opener is not the active tab, as before.
- The event `shell.editGroup` is defined in `shell-later-contracts.ts` and published from the chunk of groups, so that it is not in the start-up path; `BrowserWindowController` has no `editGroup`. The shell opens the popover once the chip shows, within a second of the request.
- The design's `tab-drop.ts` is `dropGroup` and the counts of tabs per item (`tabCounts`) in `strip-items.ts`, which the plan's tab foundations made the one module of the shell's drag code.
- The popover is an element of its own, `group-popover.ts`, as the plan decided; it has no field for the number of tabs or for a new group, as it focuses its name field each time.
- Tab search and the suggestions of the address bar open a collapsed group through `TabService.activate`; `revealTab`, which also switches the workspace, comes with workspaces.
- The access keys of the tab menu are those of the plan: `Add to new &group`, `Add to &group` and `Remo&ve from group`, in German `Zu neuer Gruppe hin&zufügen`, `Zu Gruppe hin&zufügen` and `Aus Gruppe &entfernen`, as the design's `&Gruppe` clashed with the `&geteilte Ansicht` of split view.
- A closed tab whose group is gone reopens outside every group, after the active tab, as other reopened tabs do; a closed group opens again there too, not at its old place.
- Tabs of a session that kept the same group but do not stand together form a group each, with the same name and color, so that their order stays as it was.
- The contrast test of the swatches is `app/src/main/__tests__/swatches.test.ts`, next to the test of `tokens.css`, which it reads.
- The command `tab.newGroup` comes with the commands of workspaces, as the design's step of workspace shortcuts has it.
- The workspaces add 2.0 KiB to the start-up path, not 0.6 KiB: the static walk of `out/main/index.js` with the chunks that it imports is 139.9 KiB with them, 137.9 KiB before, and they would add about 4.5 KiB without the cuts of the same step below. The design estimated 9 to 10 KiB for groups and workspaces together, which take 2.8 KiB in all. What every start needs stays on the path: the workspace of each strip and the lookups of `TabService`, the session's fields, the three settings, thirteen commands with their shortcuts, the partition of each view and the wiring of the window and of `bootstrap.ts`. To make room, the setting of the site permissions checks their limit without their code, the names of the built-in search engines load with the first new tab page, the commands of the windows leave the states of the pages out of the history of a tab that moves into another workspace, and putting a tab to sleep loads with those commands.
- Group ids are unique in their strip, not in their window: a group never spans two strips, and putting strips together, when workspaces are turned off or a session comes back, forms their groups again with new ids, so that groups of two workspaces never join.
- The operations of workspaces are functions of `workspace-strips.ts` that run through `TabService.edit`, such as `switchWorkspace`, `moveToWorkspace`, `mergeWorkspaces` and `restoreWorkspaceTabs`, and following the settings is `applyWorkspaces` of `app/src/main/workspaces.ts`, not methods of `TabService` and `WindowManager` such as `mergeInto` and `applyWorkspaces`, so that they load with the workspaces. As that code may not have loaded when the settings change, `bootstrap.ts` loads it for a window with tabs in another workspace, and its first use applies what changed before.
- The workspace of a hidden strip that tabs moved into before it ever showed has no active tab; a switch to it shows the first of its tabs instead of a new tab page.
- The default workspace may move in the list like the others; a new profile has it first.
- The drop targets lie over the start of the tabs, next to the button of the workspaces, in a row, or in a column at a side; the button and the targets count as one area, so that the pointer can go from the one to the other.
- The menu of a tab names a workspace with another partition `Name (loads again)`, in German `Name (lädt neu)`.
- Deleting the workspace that a window shows closes the list there, as every switch of the workspace closes the overlay.
- The design's code section has the menu of a tab escape `&` only off macOS, as the native menus of bookmarks once did; its section on the menu has the labels escape `&` everywhere. They escape it everywhere, as the native menus of macOS hide an `&` as well; the menus of bookmarks are popovers of the overlay since this release, which show labels on macOS as they are.

## Consequences

Positive:

- Tabs sort into named, colored groups that hide their tabs, at every place of the tab strip, with the pointer and the keyboard, and come back after a restart.
- Windows without groups never take the strip with groups, whose code loads only with the menu of a tab, a session with groups or the code of workspaces, and the start-up path grew by 0.8 KiB only.
- Workspaces keep sets of tabs per window with every page alive, build on the same strip and the same two operations of `TabService`, and use a session format that 0.1.0-beta.6 still reads; a profile with one workspace loads their code only for the list at the button, for the commands of the workspaces and `tab.newGroup`, which run in it, and at the start after an isolated workspace was deleted, to delete its folder.
- An isolated workspace keeps the cookies, site data and sign-ins of its sites apart, and its data goes with it.

Negative:

- Each grouped tab of a session repeats the name and color of its group; a group's name is at most 50 characters.
- The menu of a tab is a native menu, so its group names show no color.
- A window that had a group keeps the strip with groups until it closes; it places tabs as the plain strip does once the groups are gone, which a test checks.
- After a rollback, 0.1.0-beta.6 reads every tab without its group, and with workspaces, the tabs of every workspace in one strip; tabs of isolated workspaces then load in `persist:default`, where they are signed out.
- Each isolated workspace costs a network context and a cache on disk of its own, and the pages of hidden workspaces keep their renderers until their tabs go to sleep.
- The workspaces take 2.0 KiB of the start-up path, more than the 0.6 KiB that a part of 0.1.0-beta.7 may add.
- Screen readers hear chips as buttons inside the list of tabs, as they hear New tab there.

## Alternatives considered

- **Groups and workspaces as modules of ADR 0010:** the module host does not exist yet, and every start and restore needs them.
- **The rules of groups in `TabStrip` itself:** several KiB in the start-up path, which every start reads without a group.
- **A `TabService` per workspace:** listeners on `window.tabs` would miss hidden workspaces, and ids would collide.
- **One flat list of tabs filtered by workspace:** every operation would have to skip the tabs of other workspaces, and the units of the strip would cross workspaces.
- **Workspace definitions per window, in `session.json` or in `profile.db`:** a workspace would differ between windows, or be lost with the session, or need the data service at the start.
- **A window per workspace, as Edge does:** windows cannot share a tab strip, and Wayland lets no program place its windows.
- **Session format version 2:** 0.1.0-beta.6 would drop the whole session after a rollback.
- **A list of groups per window in the session, with ids on the tabs:** two fields to read and to keep in step, and references that can point at nothing.
- **Ctrl+Alt+digit or Option+digit for workspaces:** AltGr arrives as Ctrl+Alt on Windows, and Option types characters on macOS; Ctrl+Shift+digit is Ctrl+digit on French and Czech layouts.
